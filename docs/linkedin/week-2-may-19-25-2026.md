## Post #18 — Tuesday, May 20

**Source:** Blog Post 14 (40-Query Coverage Floor)
**Template:** Framework
**Hormozi optimization:** Hook = specific math; Retain = named four-stage structure with one sentence per stage; Reward = the named "10-per-stage floor" as a memorable label the reader keeps.
**Compliance pass:** RULE-01 (positions around measurement structure, not against the adequacy of other scans), RULE-02, RULE-10 (sector-neutral — revenue and operations both surfaced), RULE-11, RULE-20, RULE-21, RULE-22

---

The number of queries in an AI employer visibility scan matters less than how the queries are distributed.

Most of the actionable findings in an AI visibility measurement live in the variance across candidate-journey stages, not in the average across queries. A candidate forms different impressions of the same company at different stages of their decision:

Discovery — "who builds customer success software in healthcare." Category-shaped. Dominated by trade publications, vertical newsletters, consolidated rating platforms.

Consideration — "is Company A or Company B better for a senior revenue leader." Comparative. Dominated by competitor co-mention patterns and rating-platform comparisons.

Evaluation — "what is the compensation structure at Company A." Specifics. Dominated by specialist surfaces and named-leader narratives.

Commitment — "how should I prepare for an interview at Company A." Tactical. Dominated by Glassdoor interview reports, Reddit threads, named-employee posts.

The same company can be described one way at Discovery and a materially different way at Evaluation, and the difference is the finding.

Ten queries per stage is the floor at which the difference becomes visible. Fewer than ten and the within-stage citation pattern is not yet observable. Forty queries — ten per stage across four stages — is the unit of work where the asymmetries surface reliably.

Above forty, additional queries return additional resolution at a declining rate. Below forty, the findings that drive candidate behavior are present in the response surface but not yet visible to the analyst.

The coverage floor is a structural fact about the candidate-journey, not a methodology preference.

A senior software engineer asked four AI models what a healthcare-technology company builds on.

Three named the primary language, the core frameworks, the data infrastructure, the deployment platform, and the conference talks where the engineering team had presented this year.

The fourth said: "modern web technologies."

Same engineer. Same company. Same week. One conversation produced a detailed picture of an engineering organization worth interviewing at. Another produced a five-word non-answer.

Ask ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity what your engineering organization builds on. Compare what comes back.

The variance is the candidate experience — measurable, scoreable, and almost always wider than anyone expects.

## Post #20 — Friday, May 22 (LOCKED 2026-05-20 by founder)

**Source:** Inspired by Blog Post 15 (Staff Engineer journey) but reframed sector-neutral; works as standalone observation, not engineering-specific.
**Template:** Observation + reader-experiment
**Hormozi optimization:** Hook = stakes in line 1 (the candidate who passed on outreach last week); Retain = the screenshot-able "no row for…" pattern interrupt + the structurally-larger-number claim; Reward = the four-AI experiment + parallel-structure close.
**Compliance pass:** RULE-01 (positions around what the reader can observe — no audit critique), RULE-02, RULE-10 (sector-neutral — founder edit broadened "engineering" to "a certain type of role"), RULE-11 ("applicant tracking system" spelled out on first use), RULE-20, RULE-21, RULE-22. ---

The candidate who passed on your recruiter outreach last week is not in your applicant tracking system.

There is no row for "looked up the company, asked AI what they thought, read a thin answer, moved on." The leak shows up nowhere. The candidate never entered the funnel, and the recruiter who sent the outreach never learns it happened.

For most hiring programs, the missing candidates are a structurally larger number than the ones who applied and lost.

To see the leak from outside the system: open ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity. Ask each of them what they know about working a certain type of role at your company.

If the four answers vary widely, or come back thin, that is the picture your missing candidates formed before they made the decision you never heard about.

The pipeline you can measure. The pipeline leak (once you know where to look) you can measure too.
